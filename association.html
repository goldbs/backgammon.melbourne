Voting & eligibility

Community participants are not formal association members and do not have an association vote. The following summarises the supplied CAV model rules we propose to adopt; these rules are not yet in force for our association.

  • Admission: a written, signed application must support the association’s purposes and agree to its rules. Electronic signatures may be used. The committee decides applications by resolution and notifies applicants in writing; membership starts once approval and payment of any applicable fee on joining have both occurred. Community-only signup is not an application for association membership. Rules 9–11.
  • Voting eligibility: more than 10 business days must have passed since becoming a member; the member must not be an associate member and their rights must not be suspended. Rule 13(2).
  • Subscriptions: membership rights, including voting, are suspended if the annual subscription is not paid by its due date, until it is paid. More than 12 months in arrears means the member is taken to have resigned. Rules 12(4), 17(2).
  • Junior members: members under 15 are non-voting associate members. Other associate categories can be established by special resolution. Eligible members aged 15–17 may vote, but committee members must be at least 18 and eligible to vote. Rules 14, 49.
  • A voice in the association: eligible voting members receive meeting notices, may submit business, attend and be heard, vote, and access records as provided by the rules. Each eligible member has one vote, subject to the model’s meeting-specific provisions and chairperson’s casting vote. Proxies are permitted at general meetings under rule 34. Rules 13, 34, 38.

Meeting essentials

The first meeting will consider incorporation, adoption of rules, officer appointments and the membership fee. The founding meeting is planned for 1 December 2026 at 8:00 pm Melbourne time (AEDT), with in-person and online attendance available to the seven founders only. The location and calendar/joining details are provided directly to the founders.

Under model rule 30, the first AGM may be held within 18 months after incorporation. The proposed financial year is 1 January–31 December. Subsequent AGMs must be held within five months after the financial year ends (by 31 May for that year-end). Rules 50–55 provide for committee elections at each AGM, with officers serving until positions are declared vacant at the next AGM and eligible for re-election.

Notice periods · rule 33
At least 14 days for an ordinary general meeting, or 21 days if a special resolution is proposed. Notices must give the meeting details, business and any special-resolution text, and explain proxy rights.
General-meeting quorum · rule 36
10% of members entitled to vote, present physically, by valid proxy or through permitted technology. Rule 36 also sets out what happens if quorum is not reached.
Decisions · rules 38–39
Ordinary questions are decided by a majority of votes, with the chairperson’s casting vote on a tie. A special resolution needs at least 75% of members voting, in person or by proxy, to support it.
Calling a special meeting · rule 32
At least 10% of the total membership can request a special general meeting using the written, signed process in the rules.
Committee structure · rules 44, 52–53
President, vice president, secretary and treasurer are separately elected offices. The AGM decides how many ordinary committee members to elect; contested elections use a secret ballot.
Committee accountability · rules 58, 63–66
At least four committee meetings a year; quorum is a majority of committee members holding office. Material personal interests must be disclosed and managed under rule 65, and minutes and a conflict-of-interest register kept.

These are proposed arrangements under the CAV model rules, subject to adoption at the founding meeting.

Conduct, complaints & grievances

Our proposed expectations are simple: treat players and organisers respectfully, compete honestly, respect venue requirements and help newer players feel welcome.

Playing disputes should be referred to the event director. Under model rules 19–24, association discipline uses an unbiased disciplinary subcommittee and written notice 14–28 days before the hearing, with an opportunity to be heard. A subcommittee may reprimand, suspend or expel, but cannot fine a member. A suspended or expelled member may notify an appeal immediately or write to the secretary within seven days; a separate appeal subcommittee must meet within 21 days of receiving the appeal.

For disputes under the association rules, model rules 25–29 require the parties to attempt resolution within 14 days of the dispute coming to each party’s attention. If unresolved, within the next 10 days they must notify the committee, agree to or request a mediator, and attempt good-faith mediation.

The mediator must be impartial and cannot decide the dispute. If the parties cannot agree on a mediator for a dispute with the committee or association, a registered mediator is required. A grievance about a matter undergoing discipline cannot start until the disciplinary process is complete. Contact the secretary for help initiating the applicable process.

Privacy & records

The website uses registration details to maintain participation records and send verification messages. Player names and confirmed match results are public; email addresses and phone numbers are not included in the public leaderboard or event feed.

Contact the secretary about your details or a correction. Under model rules 18 and 75, formal association members can inspect the member register free of charge, subject to applicable statutory restrictions on personal-information access. Members may inspect general-meeting minutes and other records, with rule 75 confidentiality exceptions for certain records; reasonable copy charges may apply. Rules must be available free to members and applicants. Financial records must be retained for seven years under rule 70. The public website is separate from these member-access rights.
